How to use this

  1. The goal is a rebuild that a visitor cannot tell apart from the Wix site. When this guide and the rebuild disagree, the guide wins, unless a decision below says otherwise.
  2. Keep the Wix web addresses, including the odd ones such as /services-3 for the FAQ and /team-1-1 for Endorsements. Old links and Google rankings then keep working with no redirects.
  3. Copy the look, not the defects. A few things on the live site are broken, and they are listed under Decisions so nobody copies them by accident.
  4. Mobile is the one place where "exact" is not possible. Wix shows phones a fixed 320-pixel-wide layout and magnifies it. The rebuild should reproduce the mobile look with a layout that genuinely fits the phone.
  5. Blue text marked Suggested is optional. These are improvements to readability and consistency that we can offer Therese. The rebuild matches Wix unless a suggestion is adopted.
Decided October 17, 2026

Seven rulings on what "exact" means

James ruled on each of these. They override the rest of this guide where the two disagree.

1. Heading colors change from page to page. Copy them exactly.

Black on Home, dark red on About and Case Studies, periwinkle on Endorsements, navy on FAQ. The goal is that each rebuilt page matches its Wix page.

2. Replace the button font with a free lookalike.

Button labels on Wix use Lulo Clean, which Wix licenses for its own sites only. The rebuild uses Syncopate Bold, a free Google font with the same wide capitals. Every button in this guide is set in it.

3. Fix the About page overlap.

On a wide screen, the dog photo covers half of the words "About Therese" and part of the award image. The rebuild keeps the same pieces but lays them out so nothing overlaps.

4. Contact leads with Calendly, as on Wix.

The rebuild's emailed contact form comes off the Contact page unless Therese asks for a second way in. The calculator's email step still uses Web3Forms and is not affected.

5. Buttons turn red when you point at them.

Every blue pill fades to red (#ED4545) over 0.4 seconds, as on Wix. The rebuild currently turns them darker blue and will be changed.

6. Remove the Privacy Policy drafting notes.

The live Wix policy still reads "list whatever is actually running" and "(e.g. Kit, formerly ConvertKit)". The rebuild's privacy text has none of these. The live Wix page needs the same fix in the Wix editor.

7. Raise phone text to at least 15 pixels.

The homepage bullets are 10px in Wix's 320-pixel phone layout, about 12px on a real phone. The rebuild keeps the phone layout but sets no reading text below 15px.

Red team

What a senior designer would challenge

The visitor is an HR leader or general counsel with a live, painful conflict. The site has one job: make that person confident enough to book a confidential assessment. Each finding below is judged against that job. Findings tagged [Measured] come from this guide's own data. Findings tagged [Judgment] are design opinion based on common practice for professional-services sites, not tested on her visitors.

1. "Click Here" tells the visitor nothing. [Judgment]

Every main button says "Click Here," in wide all-capital letters. A button that names its outcome, such as "Book a confidential assessment," tells the visitor exactly what they are agreeing to. That lowers the hesitation right at the moment of decision. Screen readers also announce "Click Here" with no context, which is an accessibility failure.

Recommend: specific labels in sentence case: "Book a confidential assessment" and "See what this conflict costs."

2. Twenty-two colors and seven blues read as unplanned. [Measured]

Heading colors change on every page: black, dark red, periwinkle, navy. A visitor does not count colors, but they do sense when a site is inconsistent. Consistency is a large part of what makes a professional look established.

Recommend: seven colors in total, one brand blue, and one heading color everywhere.

3. Red means alarm, and the site uses it for its main button. [Measured + Judgment]

Every button turns red when pointed at, and red also sets headings on two pages. She sells calm resolution of conflict. Red is the right color for exactly one message on this site: what the conflict is costing.

Recommend: buttons darken to a deeper blue on hover. Reserve red for the cost-of-conflict message.

4. About ninety text styles in five typefaces. [Measured]

Arial, Forum, Caudex, Lulo Clean and three one-off faces. Each page invents its own sizes. The result is busy, and the blog sets long posts in Forum, a display face that tires the eye over many paragraphs.

Recommend: two typefaces and seven sizes, used everywhere.

5. The phone version is a shrunken desktop. [Measured]

Wix shows phones a fixed 320-pixel layout, magnified, with homepage text around 12px. Many first visits, especially from ads, happen on a phone. A visitor who has to pinch to read will usually leave rather than book.

Recommend: a layout designed for the phone first, with reading text never below 16px and a booking button always within reach.

6. Her strongest proof is halfway down the page. [Measured + Judgment]

"30+ years," "1,100+ mediations," and the EEOC, AAA and Los Angeles County panel logos are what make a stranger trust her. On the homepage the logos sit below the fold, after the video band.

Recommend: put the numbers and a strip of panel logos directly under the opening headline.

7. The AI avatar video may undercut trust. [Judgment]

She sells human judgment in emotional situations. Leading with an AI version of herself, even with the honest "FULL DISCLOSURE" line, asks the visitor to trust a stand-in. This is the single most subjective finding here, and it is Therese's call.

Recommend: a short, plain phone-camera video of the real Therese, or a strong photo and a quotation, if she is open to it.

8. Six filled blue tabs is an older menu pattern. [Judgment]

Each menu item is a heavy blue box, so all six compete with the actual call to action. Current practice for service businesses is quiet text links plus one prominent booking button in the header.

Recommend: text links, with the current page underlined, and a "Book an assessment" button at the right end of the header.

9. Five color pairings fail the accessibility standard. [Measured]

These include the main button label, the white headings on the blue band and the blog hashtags. Small-business websites are a common target of accessibility lawsuits in California, and hard-to-read text also quietly costs trust.

Recommend: every text color passes at least 4.5 to 1, or 3 to 1 for large headings.

10. What already works, and should stay. [Judgment]

Her voice is distinctive and confident, and nothing in the redesign should sand it down. The public "basic pricing starts at $5,000" is unusual candor that filters out poor-fit leads. "No pitch, no pressure," a direct phone line, and the cost calculator are all strong. The pill-shaped button and the warm gray and cream backgrounds are recognizably hers and can carry forward.

Recommended system

A simpler system for phase two

The current trend for trusted professional-services sites is restraint: one accent color, generous white space, a serif for headlines that signals experience, a plain sans-serif for reading, real photographs of the person, proof near the top, and one call to action repeated throughout. [Judgment] The system below applies that to Therese's existing colors so the site still looks like hers.

Buttons and links

One blue pill, everywhere

Every call to action is the same pill. It is a fixed 341 by 51 pixels, button blue, fully rounded, with no border or shadow. When you point at it, it fades to alert red over 0.4 seconds. Point at the buttons below to see it.

Voice

First person, plain, and a little blunt

Therese writes in the first person, in short sentences and fragments. Pauses are spaced dots (" . . . "). She uses quotation marks for emphasis and Title Case for headings. She repeatedly promises restraint: no pitch, no pressure.

I enable the conversations that resolve those conflicts. Then I stay until they hold.
The investigation ended. And nothing changed.
Your biggest expense is the conversation nobody knows how to have.
I don't referee it. And I don't smooth it over. I guide it.
Emotions aren't blocking the resolution. They're the roadmap to it.
Clear is kind. Unclear is unkind.
I answer my own phone and read my own email. Say it's urgent and I'll treat it that way.
Our first conversation . . . a confidential conflict assessment . . . is "real work" . . . no pitch, no pressure.

Facts she states on the site: practicing since 1992; over 1,100 mediations; based in Los Angeles; basic pricing starts at $5,000.
